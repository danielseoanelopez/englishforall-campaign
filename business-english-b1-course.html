01
Professional Introductions & Small Talk Presentarte en el trabajo y hacer small talk · Grammar: preposiciones de tiempo, lugar y movimiento
Objective
By the end of the lesson, the student can introduce themselves and their job, and make simple small talk in a professional setting, using prepositions of time, place and movement correctly.
Warm-up 5 min
  • What is your job? Who do you usually meet or talk to at work?
  • When you meet someone new at work, what do you say first?
  • What is "small talk"? When do people do it at work?

D · Grammar — Prepositions: time · place · movement

The trick: they go from small/exact → big/general.

AT — a point ON — a day / surface IN — a period / space
Timeat 3 p.m. · at noon · at nighton Monday · on May 5 · on the weekendin July · in 2026 · in the morning
Placeat the office · at the door · at a meetingon the desk · on the wall · on the second floorin a meeting room · in New York · in the building
Movement — where to?Example
to — toward a destinationI'm going to the conference. She drove to the office.
into — from outside to insideHe walked into the meeting room.
onto — to a surfaceShe put the files onto the table.
Position words (place)under next to between behind in front of near opposite
No prepositionBefore next week, last month, this morning, today, tomorrow, yesterday, and with go/come/stay home. See you tomorrow. See you on tomorrow.
US American English note
We say on the weekend / on weekends (not "at the weekend"). Also in the morning / afternoon / evening, but at night. And at work / at home (not "in work / in home").

  3. The presentation will take place ___ the second floor ___ 10 a.m.
  4. The train arrives ___ the station ___ five minutes.
  5. She walked ___ the meeting room and sat ___ the front.
  6. The company was founded ___ 2010 and moved ___ a bigger office last year.
  7. He drove ___ the event and parked ___ the entrance.
  8. We have a team call ___ Tuesday, ___ the morning.
  9. I left the documents ___ your desk ___ the morning.
  10. We're flying ___ Amsterdam ___ tomorrow.

  2. to Paris — next week ("next week" takes no preposition)
  3. on the second floor at 10 a.m.
  4. at the station in five minutes (in + future time)
  5. into the meeting room and sat at the front (also: in the front)
  6. in 2010 … moved to a bigger office (in = year · to = movement)
  7. to the event and parked at the entrance (also: near)
  8. on Tuesday, in the morning
  9. on your desk in the morning
  10. to Amsterdam — tomorrow

Model Dialogue · a networking event

Ana: Hi, I don't think we've met. I'm Ana — I work at Lumen Design.

Toni: Nice to meet you, Ana. I'm Toni, a marketing specialist at BrightBox.

Ana: Marketing — what do you do there exactly?

Toni: I manage our social media and help with campaigns. And you?

Ana: I'm a project coordinator. I work with clients from Europe and the US.

Toni: Nice. Is this your first time at this conference?

Ana: Yes, it is. I arrived on Monday, and the talks start tomorrow at nine.

Toni: Same here. There's a coffee break at eleven — want to continue then?

Ana: Sounds good. See you at the break, by the main entrance.

Q1. What is Toni's job, and what does he do?

Q2. When do the talks start?

Q3. Where and when will they meet later?

Answer key · comprehension
  1. A marketing specialist at BrightBox — he manages social media and helps with campaigns.
  2. Tomorrow, at nine.
  3. At the coffee break (at eleven), by the main entrance.
Free Practice · Produce 15 min

Role-play — the networking event. Student introduces themselves, asks the tutor two small-talk questions, and answers two back. Encourage prepositions in real sentences ("I work at…", "The event is in…", "I'm meeting a client on…").

If the student needs support, start from this frame and fill the gaps:

Hi, I'm name. I work at + company. I'm a job, and I responsibility. I usually work in/at + place, and today I'm here for + event. What about you?
Teacher note: If the student is alone, play the second speaker (Toni). Push for elaboration: "Tell me more about your role," "How long have you worked there?"
Homework
Write a short professional introduction (5–6 full sentences joined into a paragraph, not a list) about your job and your typical week. Use at least five prepositions of time, place and movement. Optional: record yourself reading it aloud and send both.

Business Meetings & Discussions Opinar, acordar/discrepar con tacto y sugerir · Grammar: verbos modales
Objective
By the end of the lesson, the student can give an opinion, agree and disagree politely, and make suggestions in a meeting, and use modal verbs (should / could / must…) to sound professional and diplomatic.
Warm-up 5 min

  • What is difficult about giving your opinion in a professional setting?
  • Why is polite, diplomatic language important in business discussions?

3 · Grammar — Modal verbs for professional communication

Modals make requests and opinions sound polite and precise. Core (B1) in bold; = B2 stretch.

UseModalsBusiness example
Recommendation / adviceshould · ought to · had betterWe should prioritize customer feedback.
Possibility / uncertaintycould · might · mayWe could explore new suppliers to cut costs.
Obligation / necessitymust · have to · need toWe must meet the deadline. Reports have to be in by Friday.
Prohibitionmust not · can't · may notStaff must not share confidential information.
Permission / abilitycan · may · might be able toYou can request more resources if needed.
Controlled Practice 15 min
Activity 1 · What is it? — write O (opinion), A (agree), D (disagree) or S (suggestion) next to each
  1. "I tend to think that launching a new product line would increase our market share."
  2. "I completely agree with that approach; expanding to new markets is a smart decision."
  3. "We might want to explore the possibility of outsourcing some production to cut costs."
  4. "That's a fair point, but I see it differently. I believe we should invest more in marketing."
  5. "From where I stand, improving customer service should be our top priority."
  6. "That aligns with my thoughts as well. Customer feedback is crucial for growth."
  7. "Have you considered a different perspective? Maybe hiring more staff would solve the issue."
  8. "While I respect your view, I'd like to offer an alternative: focus on training instead."
  9. "As far as I'm concerned, reducing costs should not come at the expense of quality."
  10. "I see your point, but we also need to consider the long-term impact of this decision."
  11. "It might be beneficial to invest in digital marketing to reach a wider audience."
  12. "I would argue that adjusting our pricing strategy would attract more customers."
  13. "Absolutely! I couldn't agree more with your proposal to improve the supply chain."
  14. "A potential solution could be a loyalty program to increase customer retention."
  15. "I understand your perspective; however, a different approach might be more effective."
Answer key · Activity 1

1-O · 2-A · 3-S · 4-D · 5-O · 6-A · 7-S · 8-D · 9-O · 10-D · 11-S · 12-O · 13-A · 14-S · 15-D

Then: pick three phrases and respond to — "Your company is discussing ways to increase employee satisfaction. What would you say?"

Activity 2 · Make it professional — rewrite each order using a modal verb
  1. "You have to fix this mistake right now."
  2. "Send me the report by 5 p.m."
  3. "We can't accept late submissions."
  4. "Tell the team to work faster."
  5. "You need to come to the meeting on time."
  6. "Don't share this information with anyone."
  7. "We have to make a decision soon."
  8. "Take care of this problem immediately."
  9. "Cancel the order now."

Answer key · Activity 2 (sample answers — accept any polite modal version)
  1. Could you correct this mistake as soon as possible?
  2. Could you send me the report by 5 p.m.?
  3. Unfortunately, we cannot accept late submissions.
  4. We should ask the team to speed up a little. / The team may need to work a bit faster.
  5. Could you please arrive on time for the meeting? / You should arrive on time.
  6. This information must not be shared with anyone.
  7. We should make a decision soon. / We need to decide soon.
  8. Could you deal with this problem right away? / This must be dealt with immediately.
  9. Could you cancel the order, please? / We should cancel the order.
  10. Unfortunately, we're unable to approve your request. / We cannot approve it at this time.
Model Dialogue · a short team meeting

Facilitator: Sales are down this quarter. What do you think we should do?

Maria: In my opinion, we should focus on our existing customers. We could start a loyalty program.

Diego: I see your point, but I'm not sure that's enough. I think we need new customers too.

Maria: That's a fair point. Maybe we could do both?

Diego: Good idea. How about a small discount for referrals?

Facilitator: I like that. It might be beneficial to test it for one month first.

Maria: Agreed. We must set a clear budget, though.

Diego: Definitely. We shouldn't spend more than we can afford.

Q1. What does Maria suggest first?

Q2. Does Diego agree completely? What does he add?

Q3. What do they decide to do first?

Answer key · comprehension
  1. A loyalty program — focusing on existing customers.
  2. No — he politely disagrees; he thinks they also need to attract new customers.
  3. Test the referral discount for one month, with a clear budget.
Free Practice · Simulated meeting 15 min

Scenario: Your company is planning to expand into a new international market. The team meets to discuss strategy, risks, and next steps. Each person gives an opinion, responds to others, and suggests a way forward.

Student picks a role: Project Manager (timelines/budget) · Marketing Lead (branding/research) · Sales Director (demand/pricing) · Financial Analyst (costs/risks).

Facilitator (tutor): "Thanks for joining. Today we're discussing our expansion into a new market. What are the key opportunities and challenges?"

Opinion: From where I stand, … could be ….
Agree: That's a good point. I agree that ….
Disagree: I see your point, but I'm not sure …. We might also need ….
Suggest: We could …. / A potential solution could be ….
Teacher note: If the student is alone, feed scripted statements for them to react to ("I think we should enter with the cheapest price" → student agrees/disagrees + suggests). Aim for at least one of each: opinion, agreement, polite disagreement, suggestion.
Homework
Write a short email (3–4 sentences) summarizing the key decisions from today's "meeting." Use two opinion phrases and at least one modal verb. Optional: listen to 2–3 minutes of a real business meeting (podcast/YouTube) and note three useful phrases.

B · Politeness — indirect language (direct → polite → more polite)

More soft phrases: "I would appreciate it if you could…" · "Would it be possible to…?" · "Could you kindly…?"

C · Formal vs. informal
FeatureInformalFormal
Modals for politenessCan you send it?Could you send it? / Would you be able to send it?
ContractionsI'll get back to you.I will get back to you.
B2 stretch · Passive voice for a neutral, diplomatic tone

Structure: be + past participle. It shifts focus to the action, not the person — useful when the "doer" is unknown or when you don't want to point a finger.

  • "You made a mistake in the report." → "A mistake was made in the report."
  • "The manager approved the request." → "The request was approved (by the manager)."
  • "You didn't send the document." → "The document has not been sent yet."
US American English in emails
Use American spelling: color (not colour), behavior (not behaviour), organize (not organise), center (not centre). Dates: April 5 or 4/5. And on the weekend (not "at the weekend").

  2. "I need you to review this document."
  3. "Can you attend the meeting at 3 p.m.?"
  4. "Give me an update on the project."
Answer key · Activity 1 (sample answers)
  1. Could you send me the details by tomorrow, please? / I would appreciate it if you could send me the details by tomorrow.
  2. Would you mind reviewing this document? / Could you please review this document?
  3. Would you be able to attend the meeting at 3 p.m.?

Activity 2 · Fix the email — identify the problems, then rewrite it professionally

Email 1 (with mistakes):

"Hey, I need you to send me the report ASAP. Also, I don't get the numbers in the last section. Explain them. Thanks."

Email 2 (with mistakes):

Answer key · Activity 2

Problems to spot: no subject line · too-casual greeting ("Hey/Hi,") · too direct ("I need you to…", "Fix it") · contractions ("don't", "won't") · imperative with no softening ("Explain them.") · no proper sign-off ("Thanks/Bye").

Model rewrite — Email 1:

Subject: Report and a Question on the Final Section

I hope you are doing well. Could you please send me the report when you have a moment? I would also appreciate a short explanation of the numbers in the last section, as I am not sure I understand them fully.
Thank you for your help.
Best regards,
[Your name]

Model rewrite — Email 2:

Subject: Delayed Shipment — Request for an Update
Dear [Name],
I am writing regarding our recent shipment, which has unfortunately been delayed. As a result, we may not receive it within the expected time frame. Could you please look into this and let us know the possible solutions and a new delivery date?
Thank you for your attention to this matter.
Best regards,
[Your name]

3 · Grammar — Conditionals for negotiation
TypeFormBusiness example
First conditional
real, possible · B1 core		If + present simple, will + baseIf you place a larger order, we will give a 10% discount.
If we lower the price, will you sign today?
unless / as long as / provided that
B1+		condition to proceedUnless we agree on the terms, we can't proceed.
We can start as long as the deposit is paid.
We'll accept, provided that you increase the quantity.
Second conditional
hypothetical, less likely · B2		If + past simple, would + baseIf we reduced the price, would you commit to a long-term contract?
Third conditional
imaginary past · B2		If + past perfect, would have + past participleIf you had accepted the terms, we could have delivered by last week.
Teacher note — a fix from the source: the "If you had accepted… we could have delivered…" example is a third conditional (imaginary past), not a second conditional. Keep the two apart: 2nd = unlikely present/future; 3rd = a past that didn't happen.

  5. If we reduce the price, will you increase the order?
Activity 3 · Correct or incorrect? — fix the ones that are wrong
  1. "If you agreed to the new terms, we will deliver next week."
  2. "If we reduce the price, they order more."

Answer key · Activity 3

  2. Incorrect — the result needs will. → If we reduce the price, they will order more.
  3. Correct. (first conditional)
  4. Correct. (unless + present, will)
  5. Incorrect — second conditional needs could/would. → If we had more time, we could explore other options.
Model Dialogue · negotiating with a supplier

Buyer: If you lower the price by 10%, we will place a larger order.

Supplier: I understand your request, but we can only offer 5% unless you increase your order by 20%.

Buyer: What if we agree to a long-term contract? Would you consider the 10%?

Supplier: If you commit to a one-year contract, we could offer you the 10% discount.

Buyer: That works for us. I'm confident this is a fair deal for both sides.

Q1. What does the buyer offer in exchange for a lower price?

Q2. Under what condition will the supplier give 10%?

Q3. Do they reach an agreement?

Answer key · comprehension
  1. A larger order — and then a one-year (long-term) contract.
  2. If the buyer commits to a one-year contract.
  3. Yes — the buyer accepts; it's a fair deal for both sides.
Free Practice · Role-play negotiations 15 min

Run one or more of these. The student uses the vocabulary, counteroffers, and conditionals. Sample flows below — the student doesn't have to copy them.

ScenarioHow it can go
1. Contract with a supplierBuyer: "If you lower the price by 10%, we'll place a larger order." → Supplier: "Only 5%, unless you increase the order by 20%." → Buyer: "What if we sign a long-term contract?" → Supplier: "Then we could do 10%."
2. Price of a serviceClient: "We could agree if you reduce the fee by 15%." → Provider: "Unless you accept our current rate, we can't proceed." → Client: "What if we extend the timeline?" → Provider: "If you give us two extra weeks, we can lower the fee by 10%."
3. Delivery timelineSupplier: "If you confirm today, we'll deliver by Friday." → Client: "What if we need it by Wednesday?" → Supplier: "We could, if you pay an expedited fee." → Client: "If you guarantee Wednesday, we'll proceed."

Support frame for the student's lines:

If you …, we will …. · What if we …? · We can agree to that as long as / if …. · I'm confident we can ….
Homework
