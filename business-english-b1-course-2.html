05
Presentations & Public Speaking Presentar delante de un grupo · Grammar: present perfect vs. past simple
🎯 Objective
By the end of the lesson, the student can open, structure and close a short work presentation, and say what the team has done (present perfect) versus what it did (past simple, finished time).
🔥 Warm-up 5 min
  • Have you ever presented something at work? How did it go?
  • What makes a presentation boring? What makes one good?
  • What do you say in the first ten seconds, before you start?

C · Grammar — Present perfect vs. past simple

One question decides it: is there a finished time word? If yes → past simple. If no → present perfect.

PRESENT PERFECT ✅PAST SIMPLE 📅
Formhave / has + past participle
We have finished. She has sent it.		verb + -ed (or irregular)
We finished. She sent it.
UseThe when doesn't matter — the result now does.A finished moment in the past.
Time wordsalready · just · yet · so far · ever / never · this week · since 2020 · for two yearsyesterday · last month · in March · two weeks ago · when I started
ExampleWe have launched two products this year. the year isn't overWe launched two products last year. the year is closed
The presentation patternStart with the perfect for status: "So far we've finished the design." Then switch to the simple for details with a date: "We ran the first tests in April."
⚠ Watch outWe have finished it yesterday. We finished it yesterday.
I work here since 2021. I have worked here since 2021.
🇺🇸 American English note
Americans often use the past simple where British English requires the perfect: "Did you finish yet?" · "I already sent it." Both are fine in the US. Teach the perfect for production (it is never wrong, and exams want it); teach the simple for recognition.
B2 stretch · present perfect continuous
For the activity rather than the result: "We've been testing the app for three weeks." · "The team has been working overtime since May." Compare: We've tested it (done, result) vs. We've been testing it (ongoing, effort).
✏️ Controlled Practice 15 min
Activity 1 · Present perfect or past simple? — put the verb in the right form

  2. I ___ (already / send) you the slides.
  3. Last quarter, sales ___ (grow) by eight percent.
  4. We ___ (not decide) on a launch date yet.

  6. So far this year, we ___ (launch) two products.

Answer key · Activity 1

  6. have launched (so far this year = still open)
  7. Have you read (US also: Did you read…?)
  8. has called (if the morning is over: called)
  9. presented (last May = closed)
  10. have just talked (also: have just spoken)

🎧 Model Dialogue · the project update

Ana: Good morning, everyone. Thanks for coming. Today I'd like to talk about the new booking app.

Toni: Great. How far along are we?

Ana: We started in March, and so far we've finished the design and the first tests.

Toni: Have you finalized the budget?

Ana: Not yet — we haven't finalized it. So far we've spent about sixty percent.

Toni: And the delay we talked about last month?

Ana: We fixed it two weeks ago. The team has worked late every day since then.

Toni: Good. So, are we on track?

Ana: To sum up: the design is done, the budget isn't final, and we're on track for October. Any questions?

Toni: Just one — when do we see the demo?

Ana: I'll send a link this afternoon.

Q1. What has the team finished so far?

Q2. What have they not finished?

Q3. When did they fix the delay — and why does Ana use a different tense there?

Answer key · comprehension
  1. The design and the first tests.
  2. The budget — it isn't finalized (and about 40% of it is unspent).
  3. Two weeks ago. That is a finished time, so it takes the past simple — while the unfinished status ("so far we've finished") takes the present perfect.
🗣️ Free Practice · Produce 15 min

Role-play — the two-minute update. The student presents a real project from their job. If they don't have one, give them this: you manage the opening of a new café — the location is signed, the menu is written, the staff is not hired, opening is planned for November. Tutor is the audience and asks one question at the end.

If the student needs support, start from this frame and fill the gaps:

Good morning. Today I'd like to talk about project. First, I'll cover part 1, then part 2. We started when, and so far we've done what. Last month we past simple. We haven't done what yet. To sum up, status. Any questions?
Teacher note: Don't correct mid-presentation — write the tense slips down and go through them after. The single most common error here is "We have finished it yesterday." Ask "Is there a time word?" instead of giving the answer. If the student is confident, run it twice and time them: second attempt must be shorter and cleaner.
📌 Homework
Record a 90-second voice note presenting your week: three things you have done, two things you did last week (with the time word), and one thing you haven't finished yet. Open with a signpost and close with "To sum up…".

B · The voicemail formula — four beats, twenty seconds
1 · Who"Hi, this is Ana Ríos from Lumen Design."
2 · Why"I'm calling about the invoice for March."
3 · What you need"Could you send me the updated version by Thursday?"
4 · How to reach you"You can reach me at 555-0142, any time before six. Thanks!"

⚠ Say your number slowly, twice. Everything else can be repeated in an email; the number cannot.

C · Grammar — Reported speech — saying what someone else said

The rule of thumb: the verb takes one step back in time.

Direct — what they saidReported — what you tell your colleague
"I am busy."He said (that) he was busy.
"I 'll call you back."She said she would call me back.
"We finished the report."They said they had finished the report.
"I can't hear you."He said he couldn't hear me.
"Are you free at three?"She asked if I was free at three.
"What time is the call?"He asked what time the call was. no "did", normal word order
"Call me tomorrow."She told me to call her the next day.
Words that shifttoday → that day · tomorrow → the next day · yesterday → the day before · now → then · here → there · this → that
say vs. tellHe said (that) he was late. He told me he was late.
He said me… He told that…
tell needs a person; say doesn't.
⚠ No backshift neededIf it is still true, you can leave the present: "He said he lives in Madrid." Both forms are correct — lived is safer, lives is more natural.
B2 stretch · reporting verbs beyond said
Each verb carries the meaning, so you say more with less:
offer + to do → "He offered to send the file." · suggest + -ing → "She suggested moving the call." · promise + to do → "They promised to fix it." · complain (that) → "He complained that nobody had answered." · warn someone + to → "She warned us not to reply." · confirm (that) → "They confirmed that the invoice had been paid."
✏️ Controlled Practice 15 min
Activity 1 · Report it — rewrite each line as reported speech
  1. "I'm in a meeting right now." (he) → ___
  2. "We'll send the invoice on Friday." (they) → ___
  3. "I can't join the call." (she) → ___
  4. "Did you get my email?" (he asked) → ___
  5. "The client called this morning." (she) → ___
  6. "Where is the report?" (he asked) → ___
  7. "Please call me back." (she asked me) → ___
  8. "I've sent the file already." (he) → ___
Answer key · Activity 1
  1. He said (that) he was in a meeting then.
  2. They said (that) they would send the invoice on Friday.
  3. She said (that) she couldn't join the call.
  4. He asked if / whether I had gotten his email. US: gotten · UK: got
  5. She said (that) the client had called that morning.
  6. He asked where the report was. not "where was the report"
  7. She asked me to call her back.
  8. He said (that) he had already sent the file.
Activity 2 · "While You Were Out" — read the voicemail, then fill in the message note

Voicemail — Mr. Vega: Hi, this is Martín Vega from Delta Foods. I'm calling about the order we placed on Monday. It hasn't arrived, and our restaurant opens on Saturday. Can someone call me back today? I'm at 555-0198. Thanks.

  1. Mr. Vega called ___ (when).
  2. He said he ___ (call) about the order they ___ (place) on Monday.
  3. He said it ___ (not arrive) yet.
  4. He explained that their restaurant ___ (open) on Saturday.
  5. He asked if someone ___ (can) call him back ___ (today).
Answer key · Activity 2
  1. this morning / today (any sensible time)
  2. he was calling about the order they had placed on Monday
  3. it hadn't arrived yet
  4. their restaurant opened / was opening / would open on Saturday all three are used; "was opening" is the most natural for a fixed plan
  5. if someone could call him back that day
🎧 Model Dialogue 1 · a video call that isn't cooperating

Ana: Hi Toni, can you hear me OK?

Toni: Hi Ana — you're breaking up a little. Could you speak up?

Ana: Is that better? Let me turn my camera off — the connection is poor today.

Toni: Much better. Is this still a good time for you?

Ana: It is. Let me share my screen — can you see the invoice?

Toni: Sorry — you go ahead. Yes, I can see it now.

Ana: Great. The client asked for a discount, so I need your approval before Friday.

Toni: Can I call you back in ten? I want to check the numbers first.

Ana: Perfect. Thanks for your time — talk soon.

🎧 Model Dialogue 2 · the voicemail, and passing it on

Voicemail — Toni: Hi Ana, this is Toni. I'm calling about the Delta Foods order — it still hasn't arrived. Could you check with the supplier and call me back before six? I'm at 555-0142. Thanks.

Ana (to a colleague): Toni called about Delta Foods. He said the order still hadn't arrived, and he asked me to check with the supplier. He also asked if I could call him back before six.

Q1. Why does Ana turn her camera off?

Q2. What does Ana need from Toni, and by when?

Q3. In Dialogue 2, what exactly did Toni ask Ana to do?

Answer key · comprehension
  1. The connection is poor / she is breaking up — turning off video saves bandwidth.
  2. His approval for the client's discount, before Friday.
  3. Check with the supplier about the Delta Foods order, and call him back before six.
Notice
Dialogue 2 is the whole grammar point in three lines: the same information, once as direct speech (the voicemail) and once as reported speech (Ana telling a colleague). Play them back to back and have the student spot every change.
🗣️ Free Practice · Produce 15 min

Role-play — back to back. Sit facing away from the student (or turn both cameras off). No lip-reading, no gestures — this is the point.

  • Round 1 — the student calls. They call the tutor to reschedule Thursday's meeting. They must open properly ("Hi, this is…"), say why, propose two new times, and close.
  • Round 2 — the tutor leaves a voicemail (read it fast, once). The student writes the message down, then reports it out loud: "He said he… and he asked if…"
  • Round 3 (optional) — sabotage. The tutor mumbles or talks over the student, who has to use the repair phrases: "Sorry, could you repeat that?" / "You're breaking up."
Hi, this is name from company. I'm calling about topic. Is this a good time? … Could we request? … You can reach me at number. Thanks — talk soon.
Teacher note: Round 2 is the assessment. Listen for three things: said vs. told me, the backshift, and asked if (not "asked that"). Read the voicemail only once — asking "could you repeat that?" is a pass, not a failure.
📌 Homework
Leave a real 30-second voicemail (WhatsApp is fine) using the four beats: who → why → what you need → your number. Then write what you said as reported speech: "I said that I…, and I asked if…" Send both.

🎧 Model Dialogue · the quarterly numbers

Ana: OK, this slide shows revenue by quarter. As you can see, we started the year at a hundred and twenty thousand.

Toni: That was our weakest quarter, right?

Ana: It was the worst of the year. Then revenue rose sharply in Q2 — up by ninety, to two hundred and ten.

Toni: And then it dropped?

Ana: Only slightly — a drop of fifteen. Q3 was a little lower than Q2, nothing dramatic.

Toni: Sorry — by how much, exactly?

Ana: Fifteen thousand, so around seven percent. And Q4 was our strongest quarter — twice as high as Q1.

Toni: So the year ended well.

Ana: It did. Revenue grew steadily overall, and the key takeaway is simple: Q2 and Q4 carried the year.

Q1. Which was the best quarter, and which was the worst?

Q2. How big was the drop in Q3 — in numbers and in percent?

Q3. What is the difference between "up by ninety" and "up to two hundred and ten"?

Answer key · comprehension
  1. Best: Q4 (240). Worst: Q1 (120).
  2. A drop of 15 (thousand), about 7%.
  3. by = the size of the change (+90). to = the number it reached (210).
🗣️ Free Practice · Produce 15 min

Role-play — present the chart. Two rounds:

  • Round 1: the student presents the chart above, from memory, without reading the sentences. Required: one comparative, one superlative, two degree words, and one by / to pair.
  • Round 2: the tutor draws a new four-bar chart on paper (e.g. 300 · 280 · 150 · 340 — a crash and a recovery) and the student describes it cold.

Frame to lean on:

This slide shows what by period. We started at number. In period it verb + adverb, up by number to number. Period was the superlative — degree higher than period. The key takeaway is idea.
Teacher note: Interrupt at least once with "Sorry — by how much?" That single question forces the by / to distinction under pressure, which is the whole grammar point. Second most common error: "the more high quarter" — drill higher / the highest in isolation if it appears.
📌 Homework
Find one real number about your job, your company or your city (revenue, prices, population, rent — anything you can check). Write five sentences comparing it to last year, using at least one superlative, one degree adverb, and one rose/fell by … to ….

🎯 Objective
By the end of the lesson, the student can receive a complaint, apologize professionally without over-apologizing, propose options using the second conditional, and agree on a fix — in speech and in writing.
🔥 Warm-up 5 min

🎧 Model Dialogue · the late shipment

Toni (client): I placed that order two weeks ago and it still hasn't arrived. We open on Saturday. This is a serious problem for us.

Ana (support): I'm sorry to hear that, and I completely understand — with Saturday coming, that's stressful.

Toni: So what happened?

Ana: I apologize for the delay. The shipment was held at customs on Tuesday. That shouldn't have happened, and it's on us — we should have flagged it sooner.

Toni: Fine, but I need the order.

Ana: Of course. If we sent a replacement from our local warehouse today, it would reach you Thursday. Would that work for you?

Toni: Thursday is tight. What else can you do?

Ana: Would it help if we split it — the essentials tomorrow morning, the rest on Thursday? And I'd take twenty percent off this order.

Toni: That works. Send the essentials first.

Ana: Done. I'll email the confirmation within the hour, and I'll follow up on Thursday myself. Again, I'm sorry for the trouble.

Q1. What went wrong, and whose fault is it?

Q2. Ana offers two options. What are they, and which does Toni accept?

Q3. Find the four steps in Ana's lines. How many times does she apologize?

Answer key · comprehension
  1. The shipment was held at customs on Tuesday; Ana takes responsibility ("it's on us") because nobody warned the client.
  2. (a) A replacement from the local warehouse arriving Thursday; (b) a split delivery — essentials tomorrow, the rest Thursday, plus 20% off. Toni takes (b).
  3. Acknowledge → apologize → explain (customs) → offer. She apologizes twice: once at the start, once at the close — and never in between, because between them she is solving the problem.
