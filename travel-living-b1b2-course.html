TRAVEL & LIVING
4-LESSON COURSE

Real English for real life on the move: making travel plans, sorting out problems where you're staying, eating out and giving recommendations, and telling the story of a trip. Built PPP style — Present → Practice → Produce.

B1 core B2 stretch 4 lessons · ~50–55 min each 1:1 or small group US American English

HOW TO USE THIS COURSE

Each lesson follows the same rhythm so students always know where they are. Use the Teacher / Student toggle at the top: Student view hides the answer keys and teacher notes so you can print or screen-share a clean handout.

Warm-up (5 min)Get talking, activate the topic.

Controlled Practice (15 min)Guided exercises. Answers in the key.
Model DialogueA short conversation + comprehension. Read it aloud, or record it for audio later.

01
Travel Plans & Getting Around Planear un viaje y moverte · Grammar: futuros (going to / will / present continuous)
Objective
By the end of the lesson, the student can talk about travel plans and arrangements and ask for and give directions, choosing correctly between going to, will, and the present continuous.
Warm-up 5 min
  • Where are you going on your next trip? Any plans yet?
  • How do you usually get around a new city?
  • What's the best way to ask for directions from a stranger?

Grammar — three ways to talk about the future
FormUseExample
be going toplan / intention · prediction from evidenceWe're going to visit Rome. · Look at those clouds — it's going to rain.
present continuousfixed arrangement (with a time/place)We're flying on Friday at 6. · I'm meeting Ana at noon.
will ('ll)decision now · offer · prediction/opinionI 'll take the train. · I 'll help you. · It 'll be fun.

Questions: "What are you doing this weekend?" · "What time are you leaving?" · "Are you going to book a hotel?"

B2 stretch
Future continuous for an action in progress later: "This time tomorrow, I'll be flying to Tokyo." · Softer plans: "We're hoping to…", "We're planning on …ing", "I might stay an extra day."
Controlled Practice 15 min
Activity 1 · Future forms — choose the natural one
  1. Look at those clouds — it ___ (rain).
  2. This bag is heavy. — Don't worry, I ___ (carry) it for you.
  3. We ___ (fly) to Lima on Monday at 9 a.m.
  4. I've decided: I ___ (learn) to drive this year.
  5. A: Are you free tonight? B: No, sorry — I ___ (have) dinner with Ana.
  6. Don't worry about the trip. I'm sure everything ___ (be) fine.
Answer key · Activity 1
  1. is going to rain (prediction from evidence)
  2. 'll carry (offer / decision now)
  3. 're flying (fixed arrangement + time)
  4. 'm going to learn (intention/decision)
  5. 'm having (arrangement)
  6. 'll be (prediction/reassurance)
Activity 2 · Directions — complete the word (Go / Turn / Cross / on / next)
  1. ___ straight for two blocks.
  2. ___ left at the bank.
  3. ___ the street at the lights.
  4. It's ___ the corner of Main and 5th.
  5. The station is ___ to the museum.
Answer key · Activity 2

1. Go · 2. Turn · 3. Cross · 4. on · 5. next

Model Dialogue · planning Saturday

Nao: So, what's the plan for Saturday?

Ben: We're taking the early train to the coast. It leaves at 8.

Nao: Nice! How do we get to the station?

Ben: Go straight down this street, then turn left at the bank. It's next to the museum.

Nao: Got it. I think I'll bring my camera.

Ben: Good idea. It's going to be a beautiful day.

Q1. How are they traveling on Saturday, and what time?

Q2. How do they get to the station?

Q3. What does Nao decide to bring?

Answer key · comprehension
  1. By the early train — it leaves at 8.
  2. Straight down the street, then left at the bank; it's next to the museum.
  3. A camera ("I'll bring my camera").
Free Practice · Produce 15 min

Plan a weekend trip together. Decide transport, times, and what you'll do — then give directions from here to the station/airport. The tutor plays a travel partner and a stranger you ask for directions.

We're -ing on day/time. First, we're going to plan. I think I'll decision.  —  Excuse me, how do I get to place? · Go …, turn …, it's next to / opposite ….
Teacher note: Listen for the will/going-to/present-continuous choice, not just correct verbs. If the student is alone, you take the partner + stranger roles.
Homework
Write 6–7 sentences about your next trip: your plans, one fixed arrangement, and one prediction. Use going to, the present continuous, and will at least once each.

Grammar — present perfect (have/has + past participle)
MarkerMeaningExample
for + periodhow long (until now)I've been here for two days.
since + pointfrom a start point (until now)The AC hasn't worked since last night.
already / justdone before now / a moment agoI've already checked in. · They've just cleaned it.
yet (?/–)up to now (expected)Has the taxi arrived yet? · It hasn't come yet.

Present perfect vs. past simple: "It hasn't worked since I arrived" (still a problem now) vs. "It broke yesterday" (finished time — use past simple).

B2 stretch
Present perfect continuous for duration of an activity: "I've been waiting at reception for 20 minutes." · Polite complaint frames: "I'm afraid there's an issue with…", "Would it be possible to change rooms?"
Controlled Practice 15 min
Activity 1 · for or since?
  1. I've been here ___ Tuesday.
  2. We've waited ___ an hour.
  3. She's lived in this city ___ 2019.
  4. They've known each other ___ ten years.
  5. The Wi-Fi has been down ___ this morning.
  6. I haven't slept well ___ two days.
Answer key · Activity 1

1. since · 2. for · 3. since · 4. for · 5. since · 6. for

Activity 2 · Past simple or present perfect?
  1. I ___ (arrive) yesterday afternoon.
  2. I ___ (be) in this hotel since Monday.
  3. The heater ___ (break) two hours ago.
  4. ___ you ___ (check in) yet?
  5. She ___ (already / finish) the city tour.
Answer key · Activity 2
  1. arrived (finished time: yesterday)
  2. 've been (since + still true)
  3. broke (two hours ago = finished)
  4. Have you checked in yet?
  5. has already finished
Model Dialogue · at reception

Guest: Hi, I'm in room 204. There's a problem — the heating hasn't worked since last night.

Reception: I'm sorry to hear that. Have you tried the control on the wall?

Guest: Yes, I've already tried it twice. And the room is really cold.

Reception: I understand. I'll send someone up right now.

Guest: Thank you. Also, has my morning taxi been booked yet?

Reception: Yes, it's just been confirmed for 7 a.m.

Q1. What's the problem, and since when?

Q2. Has the guest tried the control?

Q3. Is the morning taxi booked?

Answer key · comprehension
  1. The heating hasn't worked since last night; the room is cold.
  2. Yes — already tried it twice.
  3. Yes — it's just been confirmed for 7 a.m.
Free Practice · Produce 15 min

Role-play — checking in with a hitch. Student checks in, then reports two problems politely. The tutor is the receptionist and pushes back a little ("Have you tried…?"). Swap roles if there's time.

Hi, I have a reservation for ….  ·  There's a problem — the thing hasn't past participle since …. · I've already …. · Could you request? · Has … been done yet?
Teacher note: Reward polite framing ("I'm afraid…", "Could you…") over blunt demands. Common slip: past simple with "since" — nudge back to present perfect.
Homework
Write a short, polite message to a host or hotel about a problem (5–6 sentences), using the present perfect with for/since and already/yet.

Model Dialogue · at the restaurant

Ivy: This menu looks great. What's good here?

Leo: The pasta is the best in the neighborhood, and it's cheaper than the place next door.

Ivy: Nice. Is it very spicy? I can't eat much spicy food.

Leo: No, it's not as spicy as the curry. Do you want to share a starter?

Ivy: Sure. Let's get some bread and a salad.

Waiter: Are you ready to order?

Q1. What does Leo recommend, and why?

Q2. Is it more or less expensive than the place next door?

Q3. Is the pasta very spicy?

Answer key · comprehension
  1. The pasta — it's the best in the neighborhood.
  2. Cheaper than the place next door.
  3. No — not as spicy as the curry.
Free Practice · Produce 15 min

Two rounds. (1) Order a meal — student orders a starter, main, and drink; the tutor is the waiter. (2) Recommend & compare — student compares two real places they know and recommends one.

Can I have …, please? · I'm allergic to ….  —  Place A is comparative than Place B, but Place B has the best …. I'd recommend ….
Teacher note: Push for "than" after comparatives and "the" before superlatives. Strong students: pull in "not as … as" and "one of the best".
Homework
Write a short review (5–6 sentences) comparing two restaurants or cafés you know. Use at least two comparatives and one superlative.

Grammar — past simple vs. past continuous
TenseUseExample
Past simplecompleted actions — the main events of the storyWe arrived, checked in, and had dinner.
Past continuous
(was/were + -ing)		background scene · action in progressThe sun was shining. · We were waiting at the gate.
Both togethera longer action interrupted by a short oneI was walking to the gate when I heard my name.

Linkers: use when + past simple (the short action), while + past continuous (the long one). "It happened while we were sleeping."

B2 stretch
Past perfect for the earlier past: "The train had already left when we got there." · used to / would for past habits: "We used to travel every summer." / "We would camp by the lake."
Controlled Practice 15 min
Activity 1 · Past simple or past continuous?
  1. We ___ (wait) at the gate when they ___ (announce) the delay.
  2. I ___ (lose) my passport on the first day.
  3. While she ___ (drive), it ___ (start) to rain.
  4. They ___ (arrive) late and ___ (miss) the tour.
  5. The sun ___ (shine) when we ___ (leave) the hotel.
  6. I ___ (not / sleep) on the flight because a baby ___ (cry).
Answer key · Activity 1
  1. were waiting … announced
  2. lost
  3. was driving … started
  4. arrived … missed
  5. was shining … left
  6. didn't sleep … was crying
Activity 2 · Choose the linker (when / while / then / suddenly / in the end)
  1. I was reading ___ the train stopped.
  2. ___ we were walking, it started to rain.
  3. We got a bit lost, but ___ we found the hotel.
  4. I heard a loud noise. ___, the lights went off.
  5. We had dinner and ___ we went straight to bed.
Answer key · Activity 2

1. when · 2. While · 3. in the end · 4. Suddenly · 5. then

Model Dialogue · a trip to Peru

Ada: How was your trip to Peru?

Max: Amazing, but the first day was crazy. We were waiting for our bags when they announced they were lost.

Ada: Oh no! What did you do?

Max: We reported it, and while we were filling out the forms, someone found them.

Ada: Really? Lucky!

Max: I know. After that, everything went perfectly. We hiked, we ate great food… it was the best trip ever.

Q1. What happened while they were waiting for their bags?

Q2. What were they doing when the bags were found?

Q3. How was the rest of the trip?

Answer key · comprehension
  1. They announced the bags were lost.
  2. Filling out the forms (reporting the bags).
  3. Perfect — "the best trip ever".
Free Practice · Produce 15 min

Tell a travel story. Student tells a real (or invented) story: a trip where something went wrong and how it ended. Use past simple for the events, past continuous for the scene, and linkers. The tutor reacts ("Oh no! What happened next?").

Last year/summer, I past simple. The weather was … -ing. I was -ing when suddenly past simple. Then we …, and in the end ….
Teacher note: Model reactions so it's a real conversation, not a monologue. Strong students: fold in one past perfect ("the bus had already left") from the stretch box.
Homework
Write a short story (6–8 sentences) about a trip or a memorable day, using the past simple and past continuous together, with at least two linkers (when / while / then / in the end).
