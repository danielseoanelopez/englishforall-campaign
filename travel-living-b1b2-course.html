Grammar — three ways to talk about the future — la pregunta clave: ¿qué tan decidido está?

Las tres formas son correctas. Lo que cambia es cuándo se decidió. Ordénalas en esta escala y elige:

¿Cuándo lo decidiste?FormaSe usa paraEjemplo
Ya está agendado
hay hora, lugar o boleto		 present continuous
am/is/are + -ing		 una cita fija We're flying on Friday at six. · I'm meeting Ana at noon.
Lo decidí antes
pero no está agendado		 be going to
am/is/are going to + verbo		 un plan o una intención We're going to visit Rome. · I'm going to look for a hotel tonight.
Lo decido ahora
mientras hablas		 will ('ll)
will + verbo		 decisión del momento u ofrecimiento — This bag is heavy. — I'll carry it! · OK, I'll take the train.
El mismo viaje, tres momentos I'm flying to Rome on Friday at six. ya tengo el boleto
I'm going to visit the Colosseum. es mi plan, todavía no compré la entrada
It's raining… OK, I'll take a taxi. lo decido en este segundo
Predicciones · ¿lo estás viendo?Si hay evidencia delante → going to.
Look at those clouds — it's going to rain.
The taxi isn't here. We're going to miss the train.
Predicciones · ¿es tu opinión?Si es lo que crees → will. Va con I think, I'm sure, probably.
I think you'll love Rome. · It'll be fun.
La excepción: horarios oficiales Trenes, vuelos, museos y cines usan presente simple, como en español: The train leaves at 7:15. · The museum opens at nine. Pero tu plan personal, no: I leave on Friday. → I'm leaving on Friday.
Puente al español · en español casi todo es presente
  • «Salgo el viernes a las seis» (ya agendado) → I'm leaving on Friday at six. El presente simple en inglés queda solo para horarios oficiales.
  • «Voy a buscar hotel» → I'm going to look for a hotel. Es la que más se parece al español: ir a = going to.
  • «Te ayudo» / «Yo lo hago» (decides al hablar) → I'll help you. / I'll do it. I help you.
  • Ojo: el futuro español («viajaré», «llamaré») no siempre es will. «Mañana viajo a Lima» = I'm traveling to Lima tomorrow.

Preguntas: "What are you doing this weekend?" agendado · "What time are you leaving?" agendado · "Are you going to book a hotel?" intención

Future continuous for an action in progress later: "This time tomorrow, I'll be flying to Tokyo." · Softer plans: "We're hoping to…", "We're planning on …ing", "I might stay an extra day."

Preguntas del profe (elige cinco): What are you doing tonight? · What time are you finishing work on Friday? · Are you going to travel this year? · What are you doing for your next birthday? · Do you have plans for the weekend? · Who are you seeing this week?

Y ahora quedan: busquen juntos un día y una hora para tomar un café. El profe dice que no a las dos primeras propuestas (Sorry, I'm working / I'm seeing a client), así que el alumno tiene que cambiar el plan en el momento — ahí sale el will: "OK, then I'll come on Friday."

Teacher note: Esta actividad es el puente entre el cuadro y la práctica libre: la gramática ya no se elige en un hueco, se elige hablando. Escucha una sola cosa: ¿usa -ing para lo que ya está agendado y going to para lo que solo es intención? Si contesta todo con will (el error clásico del hispanohablante), pregunta: "Is it already in your calendar?" Al final, pídele que cuente tu semana en tercera persona: "She's meeting a client on Tuesday…" — así repasa la forma sin darse cuenta.
Model Dialogue · planning Saturday

Nao: So, what's the plan for Saturday?

Ben: We're taking the early train to the coast. It leaves at 8.

Nao: Nice! How do we get to the station?

Ben: Go straight down this street, then turn left at the bank. It's next to the museum.

Nao: Got it. I think I'll bring my camera.

Ben: Good idea. It's going to be a beautiful day.

True or false? Escribe T o F — y corrige las falsas en voz alta.

1. They're traveling by train on Saturday.  →  ___

2. The train leaves at nine.  →  ___

3. You turn right at the bank.  →  ___

4. The station is next to the museum.  →  ___

5. Nao decided to bring the camera before this conversation.  →  ___

6. Ben thinks the weather is going to be good.  →  ___

Answer key · true or false
  1. True. "We're taking the early train."
  2. False — it leaves at eight.
  3. False — you turn left at the bank.
  4. True. "It's next to the museum."
  5. False — lo decide mientras habla: "I think I'll bring my camera." si ya lo hubiera decidido antes, diría "I'm going to bring my camera"
  6. True. "It's going to be a beautiful day."
Free Practice · Produce 15 min

Plan a weekend trip together. Decide transport, times, and what you'll do — then give directions from here to the station/airport. The tutor plays a travel partner and a stranger you ask for directions.

We're -ing on day/time. First, we're going to plan. I think I'll decision.  —  Excuse me, how do I get to place? · Go …, turn …, it's next to / opposite ….
Teacher note: Listen for the will/going-to/present-continuous choice, not just correct verbs. If the student is alone, you take the partner + stranger roles.
Homework
Write 6–7 sentences about your next trip: your plans, one fixed arrangement, and one prediction. Use going to, the present continuous, and will at least once each.

Model Dialogue · at reception

Guest: Hi, I'm in room 204. There's a problem — the heating hasn't worked since last night.

Reception: I'm sorry to hear that. Have you tried the control on the wall?

Guest: Yes, I've already tried it twice. And the room is really cold.

Reception: I understand. I'll send someone up right now.

Guest: Thank you. Also, has my morning taxi been booked yet?

Q1. What's the problem, and since when?

Q3. Is the morning taxi booked?

Answer key · comprehension
  1. The heating hasn't worked since last night; the room is cold.
  2. Yes — already tried it twice.
  3. Yes — it's just been confirmed for 7 a.m.

Grammar — past simple vs. past continuous
TenseUseExample
Past simplecompleted actions — the main events of the storyWe arrived, checked in, and had dinner.
Past continuous

Linkers: use when + past simple (the short action), while + past continuous (the long one). "It happened while we were sleeping."

Model Dialogue · a trip to Peru

Ada: How was your trip to Peru?

Max: Amazing, but the first day was crazy. We were waiting for our bags when they announced they were lost.

Ada: Oh no! What did you do?

Max: We reported it, and while we were filling out the forms, someone found them.

Ada: Really? Lucky!

Max: I know. After that, everything went perfectly. We hiked, we ate great food… it was the best trip ever.

Q1. What happened while they were waiting for their bags?

Q2. What were they doing when the bags were found?

Q3. How was the rest of the trip?

Answer key · comprehension
  1. They announced the bags were lost.
  2. Filling out the forms (reporting the bags).
  3. Perfect — "the best trip ever".
