ENGLISH FOR ALL · RANK UP · CAREER

JOB INTERVIEWS
IN ENGLISH

Un curso entero para la parte que decide todo: la entrevista. Preparar tu historia, responder las preguntas clave con ejemplos reales, sobrevivir a las incómodas (fracaso, conflicto, sueldo) y cerrar bien. Estilo PPP — Present → Practice → Produce. Las consignas están en inglés; las trampas del español van explicadas en su caja.

B1 core B2 stretch 4 lecciones · ~55 min c/u 1:1 (ideal) o grupo pequeño US American English

Termina con un simulacro completo grabado y una rúbrica de evaluación de 5 puntos, para que el alumno vea exactamente dónde gana y dónde pierde la entrevista.

HOW TO USE THIS COURSE

Each lesson follows the same rhythm. Use the Teacher / Student toggle at the top: Student view hides the answer keys and teacher notes so you can print or screen-share a clean handout. This course only works with real material: ask the student for a real job ad and their real CV in lesson 1, and interview them for that job all the way through.

01
Preparation & Your Story Preparación y tu historia · Grammar: present perfect vs. past simple vs. presente para el trabajo actual
Objective
By the end of the lesson, the student can research a company and a job ad, match their experience to what the ad asks for, and deliver a 90-second answer to "Tell me about yourself" built for that specific job.
Warm-up 5 min
  • When was your last interview? What was the very first question?
  • How much do you research a company before an interview — five minutes, an hour, nothing?
  • Say "Tell me about yourself" out loud right now. We'll record it and compare at the end of the course.
Present · Language & Structure 15 min
A · The formats — know which one you're in
EnglishWhat it is · español
a screening call / phone screen15–20 min con RR.HH. — filtro: experiencia, sueldo, disponibilidad
a video interviewla más común hoy; cámara encendida, 30–45 min
a panel interviewvarios entrevistadores a la vez mira a quien pregunta
a technical interview / an assessmentprueba práctica o ejercicio
the hiring manager roundtu futuro jefe: aquí se decide
the final roundúltima ronda; a veces con el equipo
B · Before you even connect — the five-minute research that shows
Know this about themWhat the company sells, and to whom · one recent piece of news · the top three requirements in the ad · who is interviewing you (LinkedIn) · the salary range in that market
Have this on your deskThe job ad, printed · your CV · three questions to ask them · a glass of water · your phone as backup if the call drops
C · Grammar — three tenses, three jobs
Talking about…TenseExample
Your current jobpresent simple / continuousI work at Delta Support. · I 'm currently leading a team of six.
Your career so far sin fechapresent perfectI've worked in customer service for five years. · I've managed teams of up to ten.
A finished job or projectpast simpleAt Delta, I managed the night shift. · In 2024 we launched the app.

The pattern that sounds professional: perfect for the headline, past simple for the proof. "I've handled enterprise clients for three years. Last quarter I recovered two accounts worth $40,000."

Puente al español · lo que te delata en el primer minuto
  • «Tengo cinco años de experiencia» → I have five years of experience. I have 5 years working.
  • «Actualmente» → currently. Actually significa «en realidad» — si lo usas mal, suena a que corriges al entrevistador.
  • «Tengo 28 años» → I'm 28. I have 28 years. (en EE.UU. igual no lo preguntan: es ilegal en la mayoría de los casos)
  • «Me dedico a…» → I work in… (área) / I work as a… (puesto).
  • «Asistí a un curso» → I attended a course. Assist = ayudar, no asistir.
D · The 90-second answer — Now → Then → Strength → Why here
  1. NOW · where you are today (20s)"I'm a customer success agent at Delta Support, where I handle about 40 enterprise accounts."
  2. THEN · how you got here (25s, one or two jumps)"I started in retail sales, moved into support three years ago, and took over the enterprise accounts last year."
  3. STRENGTH · what you're known for, with a number (20s)"What I'm known for is keeping difficult accounts — I kept 9 out of 10 at-risk clients last year."
  4. WHY HERE · connect to THIS job (25s)"Your ad says the role is 70% retention for LatAm clients, and that's exactly the work I do best — in both languages."
If you're changing careersLead with the transferable skill, not the apology: "I spent six years in hospitality, which is where I learned to handle upset customers — that's the same skill this role needs."
If it's your first job / there's a gapBe short and factual: "I finished my degree in June and I've been doing freelance projects since then." No apologies, no long story.
B2 stretch
"My background is in logistics." · "I've spent the last three years building…" · "What drew me to this role was…" · "I'm currently leading a small team." · "That's where I do my best work."
Controlled Practice 15 min
Activity 1 · Which tense? — present · present perfect · past simple
  1. I ___ (work) at Delta Support since March.
  2. I ___ (work) at Delta Support — I started in March.
  3. At my previous company, I ___ (manage) a team of four.
  4. I ___ (handle) about forty accounts at the moment.
  5. I ___ (never / work) in a fully remote team, but I learn fast.
  6. Last quarter we ___ (reduce) response time by 30%.
  7. I ___ (train) more than twenty new agents in my career.
  8. I ___ (study) English every week for two years.
Answer key · Activity 1
  1. have worked / 've been working (since → perfect)
  2. work (el trabajo actual, en presente)
  3. managed (empresa anterior = cerrado)
  4. am handling / handle
  5. have never worked
  6. reduced (last quarter = cerrado)
  7. have trained ("in my career" = sin fecha)
  8. have studied / 've been studying
Activity 2 · Match the ad to your evidence — write the letter

The ad asks for these five things. Which sentence proves each one?

  1. "Experience with enterprise clients"  →  ___
  2. "Fluent written and spoken English"  →  ___

  4. "Strong problem-solving skills"  →  ___

Your evidence — one fits each
  • a. I've used Salesforce daily for three years, including building our reports.
  • b. I handle 40 enterprise accounts, the largest worth $80,000 a year.
  • c. I work the night shift alone, so every decision between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. is mine.
  • d. All my client emails are in English, and I run calls with US clients every week.

Answer key · Activity 2

1-b · 2-d · 3-c · 4-e · 5-a

The lesson behind it: for every requirement in the ad, have one sentence with a number ready. That's the whole preparation.

Model Interview · the screening call

Karen (HR): Thanks for making the time, Ana. Tell me a bit about yourself.

Ana: Of course. I'm a customer success agent at Delta Support, where I handle about forty enterprise accounts.

Ana: I started in retail sales, moved into support three years ago, and took over the enterprise accounts last year.

Ana: What I'm known for is keeping difficult accounts — I kept nine out of ten at-risk clients last year.

Ana: Your ad says the role is mostly retention for LatAm clients, and that's exactly the work I do best, in both languages.

Karen: That's a clear answer, thank you. What do you know about us?

Ana: You sell scheduling software to clinics, mostly in Mexico and Colombia, and I saw you opened a Bogotá office in March.

Karen: We did. And what's your English like day to day?

Ana: I can hold a full conversation — like this one. My writing is stronger than my speaking, and I study every week.

Karen: Good. The next step would be a call with the hiring manager on Thursday.

True or false? Write T or F — and correct the false ones out loud.

1. Ana currently works in retail sales.  →  ___

2. She gives a number to prove her strength.  →  ___

3. She connects her answer to the job ad.  →  ___

4. She hasn't researched the company.  →  ___

5. She says her English is perfect.  →  ___

6. The next step is a call with the hiring manager.  →  ___

Answer key · true or false
  1. False — she works in customer success now; she started in retail sales.
  2. True. "I kept nine out of ten at-risk clients last year."
  3. True. "Your ad says the role is mostly retention for LatAm clients…"
  4. False — she knows what they sell, where, and about the Bogotá office.
  5. False — she says she can hold a conversation, that writing is stronger, and that she studies weekly. Honest + specific beats "my English is perfect".
  6. True. On Thursday.
Free Practice · Produce 15 min

Build it, then lose the paper. The student writes their four beats against their real job ad, then says it three times:

  • Round 1: reading it. Fix the language here, not later.
  • Round 2: four key words only (now / then / strength / why here).
  • Round 3: nothing on the desk, timed. Target: 75–95 seconds. The tutor asks one follow-up at the end ("Tell me more about that retention number").
I'm a role at company, where I what you do + number. I started in field, moved into field when, and last step. What I'm known for is strength — proof with a number. Your ad says requirement, and that's why you fit.
Teacher note: Time it out loud and say the number afterwards — candidates always think they spoke for 30 seconds when it was two and a half minutes. Over 2 minutes is the most common failure in this answer. Cut the childhood, the degree list, and the family. If the student freezes in round 3, don't rescue them: silence is part of the practice, and "Let me start again" is a perfectly professional thing to say.
Homework
Send two things: the real job ad you want, and a 90-second recording of your "Tell me about yourself" built for that ad. Rule: at least one number, and the last sentence must mention something from the ad itself.
02
The Core Questions & STAR Stories Las preguntas clave y tus historias · Grammar: tiempos del pasado + conectores
Objective
By the end of the lesson, the student can answer the six questions that come up in every interview, and tell a 60-second STAR story — a real example with a result, told in I, not we.
Warm-up 5 min
  • Which question do you dread most? Why that one?
  • Tell me in 20 seconds about a problem you solved at work last month.
  • Did you say "we" or "I" just now? Listen to yourself.
Present · Language & Structure 15 min
A · The six questions — and what they're actually asking
The questionWhat they really want to know
Why do you want this job?Did you read the ad? Will you stay?
Why should we hire you?What problem of ours do you solve?
What are your strengths?Two strengths with evidence, not adjectives.
Tell me about a time you…How you actually behave. This is where STAR lives.
What are you looking for in your next role?Does this job match what you want?
Where do you see yourself in three years?Ambition that fits their ladder.

Rule of thumb: every answer = one sentence of claim + one example + one number. Adjectives alone ("I'm responsible, proactive and hard-working") prove nothing.

B · STAR — the 60-second story everyone asks for
  1. S · Situation — set the scene (10s)"Last year we were launching a new booking system for 200 clinics."
  2. T · Task — your job in it (5s)"I was responsible for moving the fifty biggest accounts."
  3. A · Action — what you did, step by step (30s)"First I called every account and booked a 20-minute walkthrough. Then I built a one-page guide in Spanish. After that, I ran two live sessions a day for a week."
  4. R · Result — how it ended, with a number (15s)"As a result, 48 of the 50 migrated on time, and support tickets dropped by 30% that month."
C · Grammar — the tenses a story needs
TenseJob in the storyExample
past continuous
was/were + -ing		the backgroundWe were launching a new system when the problem appeared.
past simplethe steps you tookI called every account. · I built a guide. · I ran two sessions a day.
past perfect
had + participio		what happened before thatThe client had complained twice before I took over.
Linkersthe skeleton the listener followsFirst… Then… After that… As a result… In the end…
Puente al español · la historia se cuenta en «yo»
  • En español contamos en nosotros por cortesía: «hicimos», «logramos». En una entrevista en inglés eso borra tu aporte. Di I called, I built, I ran — y deja we solo para el contexto.
  • «Estábamos lanzando» → We were launching (past continuous, el fondo). «Llamé» → I called (past simple, la acción).
  • «Logré» → I managed to + verbo, o directamente I + verbo con el resultado. I achieved to call
  • «Me encargué de» → I was responsible for + -ing, o mejor: I handled / I led / I ran.
B2 stretch
"What I took away from that was…" · "In hindsight, I'd have started the calls a week earlier." · "That experience taught me to check the data first." · "I'd handled similar migrations before, so I knew where it usually breaks."
Controlled Practice 15 min
Activity 1 · Put the STAR story in order — write 1–6
  1. Then I built a one-page guide in Spanish for the whole team.  →  ___
  2. As a result, 48 of the 50 accounts migrated on time.  →  ___
  3. Last year we were launching a new booking system for 200 clinics.  →  ___
  4. First, I called every account and booked a short walkthrough.  →  ___
  5. I was responsible for moving the fifty biggest accounts.  →  ___
  6. Support tickets also dropped by 30% that month.  →  ___
Answer key · Activity 1

c (1 · S) · e (2 · T) · d (3 · A) · a (4 · A) · b (5 · R) · f (6 · R)

Notice the shape: two lines of setup, two of action, two of result. If your story has no line 5 or 6, it isn't finished.

Activity 2 · Fix the answer — rewrite in I, and add the number
  1. "We improved the customer service a lot."
  2. "We had a problem with a client and we solved it."
  3. "I'm very organized and responsible."
  4. "We trained the new people."
  5. "In my last job we sold more than the year before."
Answer key · Activity 2 — model answers
  1. I rewrote our reply templates, and our response time dropped from 12 hours to 4.
  2. A client had waited two weeks for a refund. I called him, issued the refund the same day, and he renewed in March.
  3. I track 40 accounts in one sheet and I haven't missed a renewal date in two years.
  4. I trained five new agents, and all five passed their first-month review.
  5. I closed $120,000 last year — 15% more than the year before.

Accept any version with (a) I, (b) a concrete action and (c) a number or a clear result.

Model Interview · "Tell me about a time…"

Mark (hiring manager): Tell me about a time a project didn't go to plan.

Ana: Sure. Last year we were launching a new booking system for 200 clinics.

Ana: I was responsible for moving the fifty biggest accounts.

Ana: Two weeks in, we found that the data import had deleted some client histories.

Ana: First, I called every affected clinic the same morning. Then I built a one-page recovery guide in Spanish. After that, I ran two live sessions a day for a week.

Ana: As a result, 48 of the 50 migrated on time, and support tickets dropped by 30% that month.

Mark: What would you do differently?

Ana: In hindsight, I'd have tested the import with five accounts before running all fifty.

Mark: That's the answer I was hoping for.

True or false? Write T or F — and correct the false ones out loud.

1. Ana was in charge of all 200 clinics.  →  ___

2. The problem was that some client histories were deleted.  →  ___

3. She describes three concrete actions.  →  ___

4. She gives two numbers in the result.  →  ___

5. She blames the IT team for the error.  →  ___

6. She says what she would change next time.  →  ___

Answer key · true or false
  1. False — she was responsible for the fifty biggest accounts.
  2. True. "The data import had deleted some client histories."
  3. True. Called every clinic → built a guide → ran two sessions a day.
  4. True. 48 of 50 on time, and tickets down 30%.
  5. False — she never blames anyone. Blaming is the fastest way to lose a behavioral question.
  6. True. "In hindsight, I'd have tested the import with five accounts first."
Free Practice · Produce 15 min

Three stories, 60 seconds each. The student prepares one story for each prompt, then tells it while the tutor times it and holds up the missing letter — S, T, A or R — the moment it's skipped.

  • Tell me about a time you solved a difficult problem.
  • Tell me about a time you disagreed with someone at work.
  • Tell me about something you're proud of.
S: Last when, we were -ing background.  T: I was responsible for your part.  A: First I verb, then I verb, after that I verb.  R: As a result, number / outcome.
Teacher note: Nine out of ten learners stop after the Action — they never say how it ended, because in Spanish the result is often implied. Hold up the R card and wait; don't prompt in words. Second thing to hunt: stories told in we. A useful jolt is to ask "And what did you do while that was happening?" Keep the three stories — they get reused in lesson 04's full mock.
Homework
Write your three STAR stories (about 80 words each) and record one of them, aiming for 60 seconds. Each one must end with a number or a clear outcome — if you can't find one, pick a different story.
03
Tough Questions & Salary Las preguntas incómodas y el sueldo · Grammar: would / could hipotético + lenguaje diplomático
Objective
By the end of the lesson, the student can handle the uncomfortable questions — weakness, failure, conflict, why you left, gaps — and talk about money with a range instead of a panic answer.
Warm-up 5 min
  • What's the worst question you've ever been asked in an interview?
  • Is it rude to talk about salary where you live? What happens if you don't?
  • Finish this sentence honestly: "My biggest weakness at work is…"
Present · Language & Structure 15 min
A · The tough six — and the shape of a safe answer
The questionThe shape that works
What's your biggest weakness?Something real + what you do about it. Never a fake strength.
Tell me about a failure.Own it in one line → what you changed → what happened next.
Tell me about a conflict with a manager.Calm + how it was resolved. Never badmouth anyone.
Why did you leave / why the gap?One honest sentence, no drama, then forward: "and what I'm looking for now is…"
You seem over/under-qualified.Name it, then give the reason it works for both sides.
What are your salary expectations?A range + "what's the range for this role?"
Weakness — the pattern"I used to take on too much and then miss small deadlines. Now I block my calendar and I tell my manager what I'm dropping. It hasn't happened in a year."
"I'm a perfectionist." nadie lo cree
Why you left — the pattern"The company reduced the support team, and my role went with it." · "I'd learned everything that role could teach me, and I wanted enterprise clients."
"My boss was terrible." siempre juega en tu contra
B · Money — the two sentences to memorize
SALARY · YOUR SCRIPT
"Based on my research for this kind of role, I'm looking at a range between X and Y.
Could you tell me what the range is for this position?"

If they push first: "I'd rather hear the range you have budgeted — I'm flexible for the right opportunity."

US In the US salary is usually discussed per year ($45,000 a year), and in many states the company must publish the range — check the ad before you answer. For remote contractor roles it's often per hour or per month.

C · Grammar — hypothetical and diplomatic language
StructureExample
would / 'd for hypothetical questionsWhat would you do if a client shouted at you? — I'd listen first, then offer two options.
It would depend on…It would depend on how urgent the other task was.
I'd say / I'd probably suavizaI'd say my main strength is… · I'd probably start by asking my manager.
I tend to… habla de tu patrónI tend to over-prepare, which is usually good and sometimes slow.
should have / would have reflexiónI should have asked earlier. · In hindsight I would have tested it first.
Puente al español · las que suenan mal sin que te des cuenta
  • «Estoy de acuerdo» → I agree. I am agree. · «No estoy de acuerdo» → I disagree / I see it differently.
  • «Depende» → It depends on the situation. It depends of…
  • «Renuncié» → I resigned / I left. I renounced. · «Me despidieron» → I was laid off (por recorte) / I was let go.
  • «Sensible» → sensitive. Sensible en inglés significa sensato — decir "I'm very sensible" suena raro, no sentimental.
  • «Molesto» → annoyed / upset. Molested es gravísimo: nunca lo uses.
B2 stretch
"I'd rather not go into detail, but the short version is…" · "That's something I'm actively working on." · "My understanding is that the range for this role is…" · "I'm flexible for the right opportunity, but I wouldn't go below X." · "That's a fair question — let me give you a concrete example."
Controlled Practice 15 min
Activity 1 · Good answer or bad answer? — and why
  1. "My biggest weakness is that I work too hard."
  2. "I left because my manager never listened to anyone."
  3. "I used to miss small deadlines when I took on too much. Now I block my calendar and flag it early."
  4. "Salary? Whatever you think is fair."
  5. "I don't have experience with Salesforce, but I learned Zendesk in two weeks and I'd do the same here."
  6. "We had a conflict, but honestly it was his fault."
Answer key · Activity 1
  1. Bad — fake weakness. Nobody believes it, and it wastes the question.
  2. Bad — badmouthing. The interviewer imagines you saying it about them next year.
  3. Good — real, plus the fix, plus evidence it's under control.
  4. Bad — you lose money and look unprepared. Give a range and ask for theirs.
  5. Good — admits the gap and replaces it with proof of fast learning.
  6. Bad — blame. Say what you did to resolve it instead.
Activity 2 · Make it diplomatic — rewrite each line
  1. "No, that's wrong." (disagreeing with the interviewer)
  2. "I hated my last job."
  3. "I don't know."
  4. "I want at least $2,000 a month, that's final."
  5. "That's impossible in two weeks."
Answer key · Activity 2 — model answers
  1. "I see it differently — in my experience, the refunds usually come from billing, not shipping."
  2. "It wasn't the right fit for me long term. What I'm looking for now is…"
  3. "I'm not sure — but here's how I'd find out: …" nunca "I don't know" a secas
  4. "I'm looking at a range between $2,000 and $2,400, and I'm flexible for the right opportunity. What's the range for this role?"
  5. "That would be tight. I could deliver the core features in two weeks, and the rest the following week — would that work?"
Model Interview · the uncomfortable round

Mark: What would you say is your biggest weakness?

Ana: I used to take on too much and then miss small deadlines. Now I block my calendar and I tell my manager early what I'm dropping. It hasn't happened in about a year.

Mark: And why did you leave your previous company?

Ana: They reduced the support team, and my role went with it. What I'm looking for now is a place where I can stay with enterprise accounts.

Mark: What would you do if a client shouted at you on a call?

Ana: I'd listen first without interrupting, then apologize for the situation, and offer two concrete options. It would depend on what went wrong, but I never argue on the call.

Mark: Fair. What are your salary expectations?

Ana: Based on my research for this kind of role, I'm looking at a range between $1,800 and $2,200 a month. Could you tell me the range for this position?

Mark: We budgeted $1,700 to $2,000.

Ana: That works for me, and I'm flexible for the right opportunity — I'd want to understand the review cycle.

True or false? Write T or F — and correct the false ones out loud.

1. Ana says her weakness is being a perfectionist.  →  ___

2. She explains what she does now to control that weakness.  →  ___

3. She left her last job because of a fight with her manager.  →  ___

4. For the angry client, her first step is to apologize.  →  ___

5. She gives a salary range and asks for theirs.  →  ___

6. Their range is higher than hers.  →  ___

Answer key · true or false
  1. False — she says she used to take on too much and miss small deadlines. A real one.
  2. True. She blocks her calendar and tells her manager early.
  3. False — the support team was reduced and her role went with it.
  4. False — first she listens without interrupting; the apology comes second.
  5. True. $1,800–$2,200, then "Could you tell me the range?"
  6. False — theirs is slightly lower ($1,700–$2,000). Por eso se da un rango: los dos se cruzan entre $1,800 y $2,000, y ahí sigue la conversación.
Free Practice · Produce 15 min

The hot seat. Six tough questions, fired one after another, no pauses to prepare. The tutor keeps a straight face and says nothing between answers — the silence is part of the test.

  1. What's your biggest weakness?
  2. Why did you leave your last job?
  3. Tell me about a time you failed.
  4. What would you do if you disagreed with your manager's decision?
  5. You don't have experience with our software. Why should we hire you?
  6. What are your salary expectations?

Then swap: the student interviews the tutor with the same six, and grades the answers. Judging someone else's answer is the fastest way to hear your own.

I used to weakness, so now I what you do about it.  ·  I'd first step, and then second step. It would depend on factor.  ·  I'm looking at a range between X and Y. Could you tell me the range for this role?
Teacher note: Don't soften the hot seat — the discomfort is the point, and it's far cheaper here than in the real interview. Three things to catch: blame (any version of "it was their fault"), the fake weakness, and going silent on salary. If the student freezes, teach the time-buying phrase and make them use it out loud: "That's a fair question — let me think for a second." Two seconds of silence sounds thoughtful; ten sounds lost.
Homework
Write your answers to three questions — your real weakness, why you left (or why you're looking), and your salary range with the research behind it. Then record the salary answer: it's the one people rush, and it's the one worth money.

Puente al español · el cierre y el después
  • «Quedo atento a su respuesta» → I look forward to hearing from you. I stay attentive.
  • «¿Cuándo me avisan?» → When can I expect to hear back? — es una pregunta normal y correcta, no insistente.
  • «Preaviso» → notice period: "I'd need to give two weeks' notice."
  • «Me interesa mucho el puesto» → I'm very interested in this role. Decirlo en voz alta sí cambia decisiones: en EE.UU. se espera que lo digas, no que se sobreentienda.
  • El thank-you email casi no se usa en Latinoamérica y en EE.UU. se espera el mismo día. Es gratis y te distingue.

Activity 1 · Good question or bad question?
  1. "What does your company do exactly?"
  2. "What does success look like in the first 90 days?"
  3. "How many vacation days do I get?"
  4. "What's the hardest part of this job?"
  5. "Did I get the job?"

Answer key · Activity 1
  1. Bad — that's on their homepage. It says you didn't prepare.
  2. Good — shows you're already thinking about doing the work.
  3. Bad now — perfectly fine once there's an offer.
  4. Good — honest answers here tell you what you're signing up for.
  5. Bad — puts them on the spot. Ask about next steps and timeline instead.
  6. Good — your last chance to fix a doubt they haven't said out loud.

Model Interview · the last five minutes

Mark: We're almost out of time. Do you have any questions for us?

Ana: Yes, three. Could you tell me what success looks like in the first 90 days?

Mark: Keeping the renewal rate above 85% and learning our product properly.

Ana: That's clear, thank you. And what's the hardest part of this job?

Mark: Honestly? The clinics are small and busy, so people don't answer the phone.

Ana: That I know well — it's why I book walkthroughs by message first. Having heard the details, I'm even more interested in this role.

Ana: Is there anything about my experience you'd like me to expand on?

Mark: Good question — maybe how you handle a client who wants a discount.

Ana: I never lead with price. I ask what's not working, fix that first, and offer terms instead of a discount when I can.

Mark: Perfect. We'll be in touch.

Ana: Could you tell me what the next steps are, and when I can expect to hear back?

Mark: A final call with my director, and we'll email you by Friday.

True or false? Write T or F — and correct the false ones out loud.

1. Ana asks about vacation days.  →  ___

2. Success in 90 days means keeping renewals above 85%.  →  ___

3. Mark says the hardest part is the software.  →  ___

4. Ana states clearly that she wants the job.  →  ___

5. Her closing question gives Mark a chance to raise a doubt.  →  ___

6. She leaves without knowing the timeline.  →  ___

Answer key · true or false
  1. False — she asks about the first 90 days, the hardest part, and the next steps.
  2. True. Above 85%, plus learning the product.
  3. False — it's that the clinics are busy and don't answer the phone.
  4. True. "Having heard the details, I'm even more interested in this role."
  5. True — and it works: Mark raises the discount question, and she answers it.
  6. False — a final call with the director, and an email by Friday.
Free Practice · the full mock 15 min

The whole thing, start to finish, recorded. Use the student's real job ad. No stopping, no corrections, no restarts — 10 to 12 minutes:

  1. Tell me about yourself. lesson 01
  2. Why do you want this job?
  3. Tell me about a time something went wrong. lesson 02, STAR
  4. What's your biggest weakness? lesson 03
  5. What are your salary expectations? lesson 03
  6. Do you have any questions for us? lesson 04

Then score it together with the rubric below, and pick one thing to fix before the real interview.

What we score1–2 · not yet4–5 · ready
StructureAnswers wander or stop after one line.Every answer has a shape: 4 beats, or S–T–A–R.
EvidenceAdjectives only: "responsible, proactive".Numbers and named examples in at least four answers.
OwnershipTells stories in we; blames others.Says I did, owns the mistake, no blame.
LanguageBreaks down under pressure; long silences.Simple, correct sentences; buys time out loud when needed.
Close"No questions." Ends flat.Asks two or three, states interest, confirms next steps.
Teacher note: Record it — audio is enough — and send it to the student with the scores. The debrief that works: three things that were genuinely good, then one to change. Not five. Candidates who hear five fixes change nothing; one gets practised. If the score is 1–2 on Ownership, that's always the first fix — it's the one interviewers punish hardest and the one the student can change fastest.
Homework · course finale
Two things. (1) Record the full mock again at home, alone, with the camera on — watch it once, and write down the single thing you'd change. (2) Write the thank-you email for the interview you just practised: four lines, and one concrete detail from the conversation.
